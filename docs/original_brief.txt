Your computational hypotheses should be:

$$ H_1:\quad \text{increasing FLS causes increasing geometric deformation.} $$ $$ H_2:\quad \text{geometric deformation can occur while the representation remains injective.} $$ $$ H_3:\quad \text{at sufficiently strong feature learning, some settings may show} $$ $$ \text{injectivity-margin collapse / latent-factor destruction.} $$

And the most important:

$$ H_4:\quad \boxed{ \text{geometric untangling and actual information destruction are distinct phenomena.} } $$

You are not assuming \(H_3\) is true.

If ultra-rich learning remains injective, that is a real result.

2. Implement feature-learning strength correctly

This part is extremely important.

Do not call ordinary learning-rate changes "feature-learning strength."

3. Learning rate must be treated as a second axis

You cannot compare networks at fixed learning rate and say differences came from FLS.

Atanasov et al. show that the optimal learning rate itself changes with \(\gamma\):

for small \(\gamma\),

$$ \eta^*\propto\gamma^2, $$

while for large \(\gamma\) in a depth-\(L\) feed-forward network,

$$ \eta^*\propto\gamma^{2/L}. $$

So first run a learning-rate calibration sweep.

stable optimization;
reaches target training loss;
shortest reasonable convergence time;
does not diverge.

Once that map is frozen, never retune it during the main experiment.

4. First dataset: an exact known manifold

This should be the heart of the study.

Use

$$ \boxed{ \mathcal M=S^1_{\text{task}}\times S^1_{\text{nuisance}} } $$

which is a torus.

Generate two latent variables:

$$ \theta,\phi\sim U[0,2\pi). $$

Interpret:

$$ \theta=\text{task variable} $$

and

$$ \phi=\text{nuisance variable}. $$

Initial representation:

$$ z= (\cos\theta,\sin\theta,\cos\phi,\sin\phi). $$

Then optionally embed it into higher dimension:

$$ x=Qz $$

where

$$ Q\in\mathbb R^{d\times4} $$

has orthonormal columns.

Use:

d = 16

initially.

Why an orthogonal embedding?

Because the topology remains exactly known while the network doesn't simply receive two obvious coordinate pairs.

5. Make the task depend only on one factor

$$ y\perp\phi. $$

This is precisely what you want.

9. Your primary metrics

You need four different classes of measurement.

Do not collapse them into one score.

A. Geometric deformation
1. CKA

Compute:

$$ CKA(H_0,H_t). $$

CKA is a standard representation-similarity measure and is invariant to orthogonal transformations and isotropic rescaling.

Define:

$$ G_{\rm CKA}=1-CKA. $$
2. Pairwise distance distortion

For sampled pairs:

$$ r_{ij} = \frac{ \|h(x_i)-h(x_j)\| }{ \|h_0(x_i)-h_0(x_j)\| }. $$

Record:

median
5th percentile
95th percentile
variance
3. Effective rank

Given covariance eigenvalues:

$$ p_i=\frac{\lambda_i}{\sum_j\lambda_j}, $$

compute:

$$ r_{\rm eff} = \exp\left( -\sum_i p_i\log p_i \right). $$

This detects representation compression.

10. Local injectivity: probably your most important metric

Because you generated the manifold yourself, you know the latent coordinates.

Define

$$ F_\ell(\theta,\phi) = h_\ell(g(\theta,\phi)). $$

Compute its Jacobian:

$$ J_\ell= \frac{\partial F_\ell}{\partial(\theta,\phi)}. $$

It's an

$$ d_\ell\times2 $$

matrix.

Then calculate singular values:

$$ \sigma_1\geq\sigma_2. $$

The crucial one is:

$$ \boxed{\sigma_{\min}=\sigma_2}. $$

If

$$ \sigma_{\min}>0, $$

the representation is locally rank-preserving.

If

$$ \sigma_{\min}\rightarrow0, $$

one latent direction is being crushed.

Calculate across the manifold:

min σ_min
0.1 percentile
1st percentile
5th percentile
median
fraction < 1e-4
fraction < 1e-3
fraction < 1e-2

Don't rely only on the literal minimum because one bad sample can dominate it.

PyTorch's torch.func.jacrev and vmap are designed for batched Jacobian computation.

11. Determine which factor is being crushed

Because there are exactly two latent directions, calculate:

$$ \left\| \frac{\partial h}{\partial\theta} \right\| $$

and

$$ \left\| \frac{\partial h}{\partial\phi} \right\|. $$

The ideal phenomenon would look something like:

$$ \left\| \frac{\partial h}{\partial\theta} \right\| \text{ remains large} $$

while

$$ \left\| \frac{\partial h}{\partial\phi} \right\| \rightarrow0. $$

That is direct evidence of selective nuisance-factor forgetting.

It is much more interpretable than a Betti-number plot.

12. Global injectivity metric

Local rank doesn't detect two distant regions becoming mapped together.

So define:

$$ m_{\rm global} = \min_{i,j:\,d_M(z_i,z_j)>\delta} \frac{ \|h(x_i)-h(x_j)\| }{ d_M(z_i,z_j) }. $$

Here

$$ d_M $$

is the geodesic distance on the torus:

$$ d_M^2 = d_{S^1}(\theta_i,\theta_j)^2+ d_{S^1}(\phi_i,\phi_j)^2, $$

with

$$ d_{S^1}(a,b) = \min(|a-b|,2\pi-|a-b|). $$

This asks:

Are two points that are actually far apart on the latent manifold becoming almost identical in representation space?

Also report robust versions:

$$ q_{0.1\%}, q_{1\%}, q_{5\%} $$

instead of only the absolute minimum.

13. Better scalable collision detector

For each point \(h_i\):

find its 20 nearest neighbors in representation space;
calculate their latent distances;
look for representation-near but latent-far points.

Define:

$$ C_i = \max_{j\in kNN_h(i)} d_M(z_i,z_j). $$

Then:

$$ C= E_i[C_i]. $$

If \(C\) rises dramatically, distant latent states are becoming identified.

This is computationally much cheaper than all-pairs analysis.

14. Explicit nuisance-information probes

Nuisance probe:

$$ h_\ell(x)\rightarrow\phi. $$

For continuous \(\phi\), predict:

$$ (\cos\phi,\sin\phi). $$

Then obtain angular prediction.

Use two probes:

linear probe
2-layer MLP probe

Why both?

If linear probe fails but MLP succeeds, information still exists but became nonlinear.

If both fail, stronger evidence exists that information disappeared.

The expected pattern might be:

$$ Acc_{\theta}\uparrow $$

while

$$ Acc_{\phi}\downarrow. $$
15. Persistent homology

Now do topology.

For a torus:

$$ \beta_0=1, $$ $$ \beta_1=2, $$ $$ \beta_2=1. $$

If the nuisance loop collapses:

$$ S^1\times S^1 \rightarrow S^1, $$

you expect approximately:

$$ \beta_1:2\rightarrow1 $$

and

$$ \beta_2:1\rightarrow0. $$

But don't interpret raw PH alone as exact topology.

Beshkov's work specifically shows that metric PH can respond to geometric changes without genuine topological quotienting.

So use PH as a diagnostic, not ground truth.

30. NTK drift baseline

You also need to prove that your \(\gamma\) sweep actually crosses lazy/rich regimes.

Calculate empirical NTK:

$$ K_t(x,x') = J_{\theta}f_t(x) J_{\theta}f_t(x')^\top. $$

Then measure:

$$ D_{\rm NTK} = \frac{ \|K_t-K_0\|_F }{ \|K_0\|_F }. $$

Small:

$$ D_{\rm NTK}\approx0 $$

means more lazy.

Large drift means feature learning.

PyTorch has an official implementation using jacrev, vmap, JVPs and VJPs.

You don't need to compute this on all 20,000 data points.

Use:

128–256 fixed samples

per checkpoint.

34. Failure conditions

The project is still useful even if:

Case A
$$ \text{geometry changes} $$

but

$$ \text{injectivity never fails}. $$

Conclusion:

rich/ultra-rich representations are largely geometric rather than topology-destroying.

Case B

Injectivity collapses but PH doesn't detect it.

Then:

standard PH is insufficient for identifying representation information loss.

Very interesting.

Case C

PH changes but injectivity doesn't.

Even more interesting.

This directly confirms the geometry-vs-topology warning from Beshkov.

Case D

Everything collapses together.

Then perhaps ordinary PH works surprisingly well in this regime.

Still publishable computationally if robust.
